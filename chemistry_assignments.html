Q1 Definition of Corrosion with example 6 marks ▼
Corrosion is the gradual destruction of a metal by chemical or electrochemical reaction with its surrounding environment (air, moisture, chemicals).
Key Points
It is an undesirable, spontaneous process that weakens metals over time.
Corrosion products are usually oxides, hydroxides, or salts of the metal.
It causes huge economic loss, structural failure, and safety hazards.
Example — Rusting of Iron
4Fe + 3O₂ + 6H₂O → 2(Fe₂O₃·3H₂O)
Iron reacts with oxygen and moisture in air to form hydrated ferric oxide (rust).
Types of Corrosion
Dry (chemical) corrosion — without moisture, reaction with gases.
Wet (electrochemical) corrosion — in presence of electrolyte/moisture.
💡GTU tip: Definition + reaction equation = guaranteed 3 marks. Add types for full 6.
Q2 Dry or Chemical Corrosion 6 marks ▼
Dry corrosion occurs when metals react directly with gases (O₂, Cl₂, SO₂, H₂S, F₂) at ordinary or elevated temperature without water or moisture.
Types of Dry Corrosion
A) Oxidation Corrosion
Metal reacts with O₂ → thin oxide film on surface.
If film is stable (non-porous, adherent) → corrosion slows down — e.g. Al, Cu.
If film is porous → O₂ reaches fresh metal → corrosion continues — e.g. Fe, Mg.
Al → Al₂O₃ (protective, non-porous)
Fe → FeO, Fe₂O₃, Fe₃O₄ (non-protective scales)
B) Corrosion by Other Gases
Gases like Cl₂, SO₂, H₂S, CO₂ react directly with metal.
Ag + H₂S → Ag₂S (black tarnishing of silver ornaments)
Fe + Cl₂ → FeCl₂ (non-protective scales)
💡GTU tip: Protective vs non-protective film distinction scores extra marks.
Q3 Wet or Electrochemical Corrosion 6 marks ▼
Wet corrosion occurs when a metal is in contact with an electrolyte (water containing ions). An electrochemical cell is set up on the metal surface where some parts act as anode and others as cathode.
Mechanism
Anode parts → oxidation → metal dissolves.
Cathode parts → reduction → protected from corrosion.
Type 1 — Corrosion with H₂ Evolution (Acidic Medium — HCl, H₂SO₄)
Anode: M → Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻ (metal dissolves)
Cathode: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂↑
Example: Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂↑
Type 2 — Corrosion with O₂ Absorption (Neutral/Alkaline Medium)
Anode: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻
Cathode: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻
Fe²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂ → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust)
💡GTU tip: Always write both anode and cathode reactions — each reaction earns marks.
Q4 Galvanic (Bimetallic) Corrosion 6 marks ▼
Galvanic corrosion is a type of electrochemical corrosion that occurs when two dissimilar metals are in electrical contact with each other in the presence of an electrolyte.
Mechanism
More active (less noble) metal → anode → oxidation → corrodes.
Less active (more noble) metal → cathode → reduction → protected.
Anode: M → Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻ (active metal corrodes)
Cathode: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (less active metal protected)
Example — Zn and Cu in Moist Air
Zn (more active, less noble) → anode → corrodes.
Cu (less active, more noble) → cathode → protected from corrosion.
Prevention
Avoid joining dissimilar metals directly.
Use insulating/rubber gaskets between different metals.
Select metals close in EMF series to minimize galvanic effect.
💡GTU tip: Greater the difference in electrode potential → faster galvanic corrosion.
Q5 Concentration Cell Corrosion 6 marks ▼
Concentration cell corrosion is a type of electrochemical corrosion that occurs when two areas of the same metal are exposed to an electrolyte of different concentrations (different O₂ or ion concentration).
Mechanism
Region with lower O₂ concentration → acts as anode → corrodes.
Region with higher O₂ concentration → acts as cathode → protected.
Corrosion occurs due to difference in ion or oxygen concentration in electrolyte.
Reactions
Anode: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻
Cathode: ½O₂ + H₂O + 2e⁻ → 2OH⁻
Fe²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂ → Rust
Types
Oxygen concentration cell (most common)
Ion concentration cell
💡GTU tip: Link to pitting and waterline corrosion — they are real-life examples of concentration cell corrosion.
Q6 Pitting Corrosion & Waterline Corrosion 6 marks ▼
Pitting Corrosion
Parts covered by rust, scale, or dirt get less O₂ → act as anode → corrode.
Uncovered parts with more O₂ → cathode → protected.
Once pit forms, inside becomes acidic → accelerates further corrosion rapidly.
Results in formation of pin-holes, pits, and cavities in metal surface.
Anode: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻
Cathode: H₂O + ½O₂ + 2e⁻ → 2OH⁻
∴ Fe²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂ → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust)
Waterline Corrosion
Occurs at liquid-air boundary in steel tanks or pipes with stagnant water.
O₂ concentration above waterline > below waterline → concentration cell formed.
Metal just below waterline (less O₂) → anode → corrodes.
Metal above waterline (more O₂) → cathode → unaffected.
At anode: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻
At cathode: 4H⁺ + O₂ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O
💡GTU tip: Draw a simple sketch of pit or waterline level to earn diagram marks.
Q7 Factors Affecting Corrosion & Prevention Methods 6 marks ▼
Factors Affecting Rate of Corrosion
Nature of surface film (porous or non-porous)
pH of solution (acidic pH < 7 → more corrosive)
Area of anode & cathode (large cathode → more corrosion)
Temperature (higher temp → more corrosion)
Moisture (more humidity → more corrosion)
Purity of metal (impure metal corrodes faster)
Methods of Prevention
Modification of environment: Dehumidification using silica gel; use of inhibitors (anodic, cathodic, organic).
Modification of metal: Purification; alloying with Cr, Ni, Mo (e.g. stainless steel = Fe + Cr + Ni).
Protective coatings: Galvanizing (Zn on Fe), tinning (Sn on Fe), electroplating, metal spraying, cladding, paints, enamels, varnishes.
Cathodic protection: Sacrificial anode method (Zn, Al, Mg) or impressed current method.
Design modification: Avoid crevices, ensure proper drainage, separate dissimilar metals using insulators.
💡GTU tip: Write at least 5 prevention methods — 1 line each. Mention galvanization and sacrificial anode specifically.

Q1 Lubricants & Lubrication — Functions of Lubricants 6 marks ▼
A lubricant is a substance applied between two moving or sliding surfaces to reduce friction and wear. The process of applying lubricant between two surfaces to reduce friction is called lubrication.
Functions of Lubricants
Reduce friction: Form a thin film between moving surfaces → prevents direct metal-to-metal contact.
Reduce wear & tear: Prevent abrasion of surfaces, increasing machine life.
Cooling effect: Carry away heat generated due to friction.
Prevent corrosion: Form a protective film that prevents contact with moisture and air.
Reduce power consumption: Less friction → less energy needed to run machinery.
Noise reduction: Cushions the impact between parts → quieter operation.
Sealing effect: Lubricant fills gaps in engine cylinders → prevents gas leakage.
💡GTU tip: Write at least 5 functions — each one-liner scores marks.
Q2 Mechanism of Lubrication — Fluid & Boundary 6 marks ▼
A) Fluid Film (Hydrodynamic) Lubrication
A thick continuous film of lubricant completely separates the two surfaces.
No direct metal-to-metal contact occurs.
Friction depends on viscosity of lubricant — higher viscosity → thicker film.
Suitable for: high-speed, light-load machinery like bearings, shafts.
Example: Engine oil in journal bearings.
B) Boundary Lubrication
Only a very thin (monomolecular) film of lubricant is present between the surfaces.
Partial metal-to-metal contact may occur under heavy load or slow speed.
Friction depends on chemical nature of lubricant, not viscosity.
Suitable for: slow-moving, heavily loaded surfaces like gears and cutting tools.
Example: Grease used in slow-moving gear systems.
💡GTU tip: Fluid = viscosity matters; Boundary = chemical nature matters — this difference is key.
Q3 Classification of Lubricants with Examples 6 marks ▼
Lubricants are classified into four types based on their physical state: Solid, Semi-solid, Liquid, and Synthetic.
1. Solid Lubricants
Used where liquid lubricants cannot be applied (extreme temp, vacuum, high load).
Examples: Graphite, Molybdenum disulfide (MoS₂), Talc, Mica, Wax.
Graphite: used in locks, hinges, open gears at high temperature.
2. Semi-solid Lubricants (Greases)
Formed by mixing mineral oil with metallic soaps (Ca, Li, Na soaps).
Used where oil tends to drip or leak — slow speed, heavy load.
Example: Calcium grease, Lithium grease used in wheel bearings, pumps.
3. Liquid Lubricants (Oils)
Mineral oils: Derived from petroleum — engine oil, gear oil.
Animal/vegetable oils: Castor oil, lard oil — biodegradable.
4. Synthetic Lubricants
Chemically synthesized — stable over wide temperature range.
Example: Silicone oil, polyesters, phosphate esters — used in jet engines, aerospace.
💡GTU tip: 4 types with one example each = full 6 marks.
Q4 Physical & Chemical Properties of Lubricants 6 marks ▼
Physical Properties
Viscosity: Resistance to flow. Most important property — too thick or too thin is bad. Measured in centipoise (cP).
Viscosity Index (VI): Rate of change of viscosity with temperature. High VI = viscosity changes less with temperature = better lubricant.
Flash point: Lowest temperature at which oil vapour catches fire. Should be high for safety.
Fire point: Temperature at which oil itself burns. Always higher than flash point.
Pour point: Lowest temperature at which oil just flows. Should be low for cold-climate use.
Cloud point: Temperature at which oil becomes cloudy (wax separates).
Chemical Properties
Oxidation stability: Ability to resist oxidation. Good lubricant should not oxidize easily.
Acid value: Amount of free acids present. Low acid value = better lubricant (acids cause corrosion).
Saponification value: Measures fatty oil content — important for blended lubricants.
💡GTU tip: Viscosity + Flash point + Pour point = 3 guaranteed mark-fetching properties. Always explain each briefly.
Q5 Selection of Lubricants for Different Machinery 6 marks ▼
Selection of a lubricant depends on the nature of machinery, load, speed, temperature, and operating conditions.
1. Gears
Heavy load, slow speed → heavy-bodied (high viscosity) oil or grease.
Extreme pressure (EP) additives added to prevent metal welding under high load.
Example: Gear oil, lithium grease.
2. Cutting Tools
High friction + heat generation → cutting fluid (soluble oil in water) or cooling oil.
Acts as both lubricant and coolant — reduces heat and prevents tool damage.
Example: Soluble cutting oil, sulfurized oil.
3. Steam Turbines
High speed, moderate load → low viscosity, highly refined mineral oil.
Oil must resist oxidation and water contamination (steam environment).
Example: Turbine oil (ISO VG 32 or 46).
4. Transformers
Need electrical insulating oil — low viscosity, high dielectric strength, high flash point.
Oil also acts as coolant, carrying heat away from coils.
Example: Transformer oil (mineral oil, highly refined).
💡GTU tip: For each machine — mention type of lubricant + one reason why = full marks.
Q6 Biodegradable Lubricants 6 marks ▼
Biodegradable lubricants are lubricants that can be decomposed naturally by microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) in the environment. They are eco-friendly alternatives to conventional petroleum-based lubricants.
Why Biodegradable?
Conventional mineral oils are toxic to environment and do not decompose easily.
Biodegradable lubricants reduce soil and water pollution.
Types / Base Stocks
Vegetable oils: Rapeseed oil (canola), sunflower oil, soybean oil — most widely used.
Synthetic esters: Good temperature stability + biodegradable — used in aviation, agriculture.
Polyalkylene glycols (PAG): Water-soluble, used in food-grade machinery.
Properties
High viscosity index (VI) — stable over wide temperature range.
Good lubrication performance, low toxicity, renewable source.
Limitation: Lower thermal stability and shorter shelf life than mineral oils.
Applications
Agriculture (tractors), forestry equipment, marine vessels, food industry machinery.
💡GTU tip: Mention rapeseed oil as example + eco-friendly advantage = easy marks.

Q1 Definitions: Monomer, Polymer, Polymerization, Degree of Polymerization, Repeating Unit 6 marks ▼
Five key definitions — each with a short example earns full marks in GTU.
1. Monomer
Small, simple molecules that can join together repeatedly to form a polymer.
Example: Ethylene (CH₂=CH₂), Vinyl chloride (CH₂=CHCl), Styrene.
2. Polymer
Large molecules (macromolecules) formed by joining many monomer units through chemical bonds.
Example: Polyethylene (−CH₂−CH₂−)ₙ, PVC, Nylon, Bakelite.
3. Polymerization
The chemical process by which monomers are joined together to form a polymer.
nCH₂=CH₂ → (−CH₂−CH₂−)ₙ (polymerization of ethylene)
4. Degree of Polymerization (n)
The number of repeating units (monomer units) present in a polymer chain.
Higher degree of polymerization → higher molecular weight → stronger polymer.
n = Molecular weight of polymer / Molecular weight of monomer
5. Repeating Unit (Mer)
The smallest structural unit that repeats along the polymer chain.
In polyethylene: repeating unit is −CH₂−CH₂−
💡GTU tip: 5 definitions × 1.2 marks each = 6 marks. One example per definition is enough.
Q2 Classification of Polymers — Molecular Structure, Monomer & Thermal Behaviour 6 marks ▼
A) Based on Molecular Structure
Linear polymers: Monomers joined in a straight chain. Soft, flexible, high melting point. Example: HDPE, Nylon, PVC.
Branched polymers: Main chain with side branches. Less dense, lower melting point. Example: LDPE.
Cross-linked (network) polymers: 3D network structure with covalent bonds. Hard, rigid, brittle. Example: Bakelite, Melamine, Vulcanized rubber.
B) Based on Monomer (Source)
Homopolymer: Made from only one type of monomer. Example: Polyethylene (only ethylene).
Copolymer: Made from two or more different monomers. Example: Nylon 6,6 (hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid).
C) Based on Thermal Behaviour
Thermoplastics: Soften on heating, harden on cooling — reversible. Can be reshaped. Example: PVC, Polyethylene, Polystyrene.
Thermosetting plastics: Permanently harden on heating — irreversible. Cannot be reshaped. Example: Bakelite, Epoxy resin, Urea formaldehyde.
💡GTU tip: 3 classification types × 2 marks each = 6. Always write examples.
Q3 Types of Polymerization — Addition & Condensation 6 marks ▼
A) Addition Polymerization (Chain-growth)
Monomers with double bonds (C=C) open up and join together without loss of any atom or molecule.
Molecular weight of polymer = sum of molecular weights of all monomers.
Three steps: Initiation → Propagation → Termination.
nCH₂=CH₂ → (−CH₂−CH₂−)ₙ (Polyethylene)
nCH₂=CHCl → (−CH₂−CHCl−)ₙ (PVC)
Examples: Polyethylene, PVC, Polystyrene, PTFE, Polypropylene.
B) Condensation Polymerization (Step-growth)
Monomers with two functional groups react together with loss of small molecules (H₂O, HCl, NH₃).
Polymer molecular weight < sum of monomer weights due to elimination of small molecules.
n(H₂N−R−NH₂) + n(HOOC−R'−COOH) → Nylon + nH₂O
Examples: Nylon, Bakelite, Polyester, Epoxy resin, PHBV.
Key Differences
Addition → no byproduct lost; Condensation → small molecule (H₂O, HCl) released.
Addition → needs double bond in monomer; Condensation → needs bifunctional groups.
💡GTU tip: Write reactions for both types + one difference = 6 marks secured.
Q4 Preparation, Properties & Uses of Important Polymers 6 marks ▼
Polyethylene (PE)
nCH₂=CH₂ → (−CH₂−CH₂−)ₙ
Properties: Flexible, tough, chemically inert, low density (LDPE), high density (HDPE).
Uses: Packaging films, carry bags, pipes, bottles, insulation of cables.
Polypropylene (PP)
nCH₂=CHCH₃ → (−CH₂−CH(CH₃)−)ₙ
Properties: Rigid, high melting point, excellent chemical resistance.
Uses: Ropes, fibers, containers, automotive parts, carpets.
PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)
nCH₂=CHCl → (−CH₂−CHCl−)ₙ
Properties: Rigid or flexible (with plasticizer), good chemical resistance.
Uses: Pipes, water tanks, flooring, electrical cable insulation, raincoats.
PTFE (Teflon — Polytetrafluoroethylene)
nCF₂=CF₂ → (−CF₂−CF₂−)ₙ
Properties: Non-stick, highest chemical resistance, withstands −200°C to +260°C.
Uses: Non-stick cookware, gaskets, seals, surgical implants.
Polystyrene (PS)
Properties: Transparent, rigid, brittle, good electrical insulator.
Uses: Disposable cups, packaging (thermocol/foam), toys, CD cases.
Bakelite (Phenol Formaldehyde)
Properties: Hard, rigid, thermosetting, excellent electrical insulator, heat resistant.
Uses: Electrical switches, plugs, handles, radio cabinets.
💡GTU tip: Preparation reaction + 2 properties + 2 uses for each polymer = full marks.
Q5 PHBV & Nylon-2 / Nylon-6 — Chemical Composition & Uses 6 marks ▼
PHBV — Poly(β-HydroxyButyrate-co-β-hydroxy Valerate)
PHBV is a biodegradable, biocompatible copolymer produced by bacteria (Ralstonia eutropha).
It is a copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid (HB) and 3-hydroxypentanoic acid (HV).
Properties
Biodegradable, biocompatible, thermoplastic, non-toxic.
Similar properties to polypropylene.
Uses of PHBV
Biodegradable packaging, disposable cups, surgical sutures, drug delivery, implants.
Nylon-2-Nylon-6 (Copolyamide)
A copolymer of glycine (Nylon-2 unit) and aminocaproic acid (Nylon-6 unit) — condensation polymerization.
n(H₂N−CH₂−COOH) + n(H₂N−(CH₂)₅−COOH) → Copolymer + nH₂O
Properties
Strong, elastic, biodegradable, biocompatible.
Uses
Biomedical applications — surgical sutures, tissue engineering, drug delivery systems.
💡GTU tip: PHBV = biodegradable plastic made by bacteria. Nylon-2-Nylon-6 = biomedical copolymer. These two facts alone score big marks.
Q6 Natural Rubber & Its Properties 6 marks ▼
Natural rubber is a natural polymer obtained from the latex of the rubber tree (Hevea brasiliensis). It is also known as cis-polyisoprene.
Chemical Structure
Monomer: Isoprene (CH₂=C(CH₃)−CH=CH₂)
Polymer: (−CH₂−C(CH₃)=CH−CH₂−)ₙ — cis configuration
Properties of Natural Rubber
Elasticity: Can be stretched to 10× its original length and returns to original shape.
Flexibility: Soft and flexible at room temperature.
Electrical insulator: Good insulation properties.
Water resistance: Insoluble in water — impermeable to water and air.
Solubility: Soluble in organic solvents like benzene, ether, CS₂.
Disadvantages (Raw Rubber)
Becomes sticky in summer, brittle in winter.
Low tensile strength, poor resistance to chemicals and oxidation.
These disadvantages are overcome by Vulcanization.
💡GTU tip: Write cis-polyisoprene structure + 4 properties + mention vulcanization connection = 6 marks.
Q7 Vulcanization of Rubber & Its Advantages 6 marks ▼
Vulcanization is the process of heating raw rubber with sulfur (2–3%) at 100–180°C to improve its properties. Cross-links of −S−S− bridges are formed between polymer chains.
Process
Raw rubber + Sulfur (2–3%) → heated at 100–180°C → cross-linked rubber.
Sulfur atoms form covalent cross-links (−S−S−) between adjacent polymer chains.
More sulfur → harder rubber (ebonite with 30–40% sulfur).
Rubber−CH₂ + S → Rubber−CH₂−S−S−CH₂−Rubber (cross-links formed)
Advantages of Vulcanization
High tensile strength: Cross-links prevent chain slippage → stronger rubber.
Better elasticity: Stretches uniformly and recovers shape completely.
Heat resistance: Does not become sticky in hot weather.
Chemical resistance: Resists attack by acids, alkalis, and oxidizing agents.
No temperature sensitivity: Maintains properties over a wide range of temperature.
Reduced water absorption: Becomes less permeable to moisture.
Applications
Tyres, tubes, belts, hoses, shoes, gloves, erasers, seals.
💡GTU tip: Definition + sulfur cross-link concept + 5 advantages = full marks. Mention ebonite for bonus.
Q8 Classification & Applications of Insulating Materials 6 marks ▼
Insulating materials (insulators) are substances that do not allow the flow of electricity, heat, or sound through them. In electrical engineering, they prevent leakage of current.
Classification of Insulating Materials
A) Based on Physical State
Solid insulators: Rubber, Bakelite, Mica, Glass, Porcelain, PTFE, PVC.
Liquid insulators: Transformer oil, Cable oil, Varnish, Synthetic resins.
Gaseous insulators: Air, Nitrogen (N₂), SF₆ (sulfur hexafluoride).
B) Based on Composition
Organic insulators: Rubber, Paper, Cellulose, Cotton, Silk.
Inorganic insulators: Mica, Glass, Asbestos, Porcelain, Ceramic.
Synthetic insulators: Bakelite, PTFE (Teflon), PVC, Epoxy resins.
Properties of Good Insulating Material
High electrical resistance (dielectric strength).
High mechanical strength, low moisture absorption, heat resistance.
Applications
Rubber: Cable insulation, gloves, switchboards.
Mica: Capacitors, motor windings, heating elements (withstands high temp).
Glass/Porcelain: Overhead power line insulators, spark plugs.
PTFE (Teflon): High-frequency cable insulation, circuit boards.
Transformer oil: Insulation and cooling in transformers.
💡GTU tip: 3 classification types + 4–5 applications = guaranteed 6 marks.
